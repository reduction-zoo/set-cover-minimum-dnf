For the hard branch, the recurrence at [`algorithm.py:29-75`](../../work/algorithm.py) represents “at least `j` of the first `i` choices”; its bi-implication Tseitin clauses force the overflow value. Source element clauses and `¬overflow` are satisfiable exactly for covers of size at most `k`. Every source variable occurs in this circuit even if its input set is empty, so `wheel_true[j+1]` is defined for each source index.

The wheel and clause triples at [`algorithm.py:78-120`](../../work/algorithm.py) implement the argument in [`proof.md:19-25`](../../work/proof.md). Every occurrence has one chosen wheel triple; the cyclic `b` points force a consistent mode. Exactly one clause triple consumes its clause pair and an available tip, hence a true literal; the remaining available tips match the local garbage pairs. There are `6R` distinct points and every chosen triple has three distinct points, so a cover with at most `2R` triples is exact. Conversely, a satisfying assignment extends to such a cover. This includes unit clauses and repeated occurrences of a variable in different clauses.

The accepted list at [`algorithm.py:123-139`](../../work/algorithm.py) is precisely the sparse downset and parity completion in [`proof.md:27-35`](../../work/proof.md). A source cover yields an exact target DNF with at most `s+2R` terms. For any valid target DNF, each of the `s` parity points needs its own term fixing all original variables; none can cover a one-hot `11` point. Each term covering a one-hot `11` point fixes both auxiliary bits positively, and its maximal original vector lies under an intermediary triple. Thus at most `2R` triples cover all `6R` points, forcing an exact cover. This proves the reverse implication for every valid DNF, including duplicate or redundant terms. If a target output is `NO-SOLUTION`, the forward implication proves that the source is infeasible.

The implemented decoder at [`algorithm.py:142-169`](../../work/algorithm.py) selects precisely terms able to cover a one-hot `11` point: a kept term has no negative auxiliary literal, at most one positive original literal, and at least one original coordinate not fixed negative. Such a term indeed covers a one-hot `11` point; a term covering one also passes the filters. The mask formed from original entries other than `-1` is its maximal original vector and is a subset of at least one constructed triple, so the dictionary lookup is defined. Every kept term maps to a triple covering every one-hot point it covers. The recovered triple set has at most `2R` members and covers all `6R` points, hence is an exact cover. Its wheel markers give a satisfying CNF assignment; the first source-set variables yield a valid `cover` of length at most `k`. The guard at line 163 is redundant but harmless.

All maps are deterministic. In the hard branch, every source element and `k` are bounded by the explicit input length after the easy checks; the cardinality circuit has `O(mk)` gates, `R` is polynomial, the intermediary has `6R` points and polynomially many triples, and each triple adds at most four nonsingleton downset masks. The constructed accepted strings have length `6R+2`, and `K` has logarithmic bit length. Reconstruction, scanning target terms, mask operations, and witness output are polynomial in `|x|+|y|`; Python integers do not hide superpolynomial bit cost here. The full injected runs in [`rounds/002/candidate-run.log`](../../rounds/002/candidate-run.log) and [`rounds/002/verify-run.log`](../../rounds/002/verify-run.log) support the implementation on finite instances, including hard YES and NO inputs, distinct valid DNFs, and 61 infeasible outputs. ## Significance and overhead

The fixed question asks for an executable two-map bridge with recovery from every valid exact DNF output; this candidate supplies that bridge and a concrete size proof for the explicit accepted-list encoding. Its contribution is a reproducible reconstruction and decoder, rather than a new DNF-hardness theorem. The dominant overhead is the CNF cardinality circuit, local clause garbage triples, and accepted strings of length `N+2` for `O(T)` sparse downset points, giving roughly `O((N+T)N)` construction/output work plus polynomial sorting and integer costs. The retained run reaches 1,838 variables and 14,870 accepted assignments on a small source case, so practical minimization claims would be unsupported. No question-specific resource bound is violated.
